That insert holds a clear excess of carboxylates over basic residues, and most of the histidines in the whole protein sit inside it although it is much the smaller part of the sequence. Imidazole titrates across precisely the range that separates a mildly acidic lumen from a neutral cytosol, and carboxylates in such a lumen sit near their own pKa, so this stretch is built to change its surface charge exactly where an acidifying organelle meets the cytoplasm. From the fold, not the chain, I know that in the mucolipins this insert tetramerises into a ring seated over the outer mouth of the channel, which fixes the stoichiometry at four copies of the pore module on a single conduction pathway and makes the working unit a cation channel complex, not a monomer. A pH-titratable ring over that mouth would be wasted on a cell-surface channel and is only useful as a luminal sensor, so it places this tetramer in the lysosomal membrane and the late endosome membrane. Clathrin adaptors read only the cytosolic face, so of the three dileucine motifs the one sitting in the luminal insert is unreadable however good it looks. That leaves one at each terminus, both cytosolic and both exposed, and redundant signals at opposite ends of a single polypeptide read as sorting and never as coincidence. I treat the carboxyl copy as an internalisation signal for retrieval by AP-2 from the plasma membrane, which a channel that must not open at the cell surface will need, and the pair together as substrates for AP-1 and AP-3 delivery from the trans-Golgi and endosomes. The run of cysteines just upstream of that carboxyl motif I call a likely palmitoylation cluster, since acylation beside a sorting signal is a standard way of holding a tail against the bilayer and modulating how easily an adaptor reaches it.

On the cytosolic side, that basic surface lies against the inner leaflet and is a headgroup-binding site for a polyanionic phosphoinositide. The phosphoinositide that marks the cytosolic face of late endosomes and lysosomes is phosphatidylinositol 3,5-bisphosphate, so what the site does is phosphatidylinositol-3,5-bisphosphate binding, and a channel whose activating ligand is the identity lipid of one compartment can open only where that compartment is. The luminal cap and the gating lipid therefore reach the same membrane by independent routes. The lipid site sits far from the pore, so I expect activation to be allosteric, propagating through the cytosolic loop between the second and third crossings and through the linker that follows the pore onto the last helix, whose uncharged length is the gate. A pore that a phosphoinositide opens is a phosphatidylinositol-3,5-bisphosphate-sensitive cation channel. Which way it conducts is set by electrochemistry and not by the protein. An acidic degradative organelle holds calcium orders of magnitude above the cytosol, so the gradient across this membrane runs lumen to cytosol and opening releases calcium into a thin shell at the organelle surface instead of taking it up, which makes this calcium channel activity. Two carboxylates in the filter buy charge selectivity and a bias toward divalents, but they are not the rigid four-carboxylate ring that builds a strictly calcium-selective pore, so I stop short of high selectivity. The same conduit should therefore pass the other divalents such a compartment concentrates, the ferrous iron and zinc liberated as ferritin and metalloproteins are broken down, giving it ferrous iron transmembrane transporter activity and zinc ion transmembrane transporter activity alongside the calcium.

Opening this channel empties a loaded store on demand, which is release of sequestered calcium ion into cytosol, and a gate that decides when a compartment discharges makes the protein part of intracellular calcium ion homeostasis. A shell that thin acts only on what already sits at that surface, so fusion along the endocytic route is the first consequence I reach, homotypic between like compartments and heterotypic when a late endosome meets a lysosome, and the same trigger serves the fusion of an autophagosome with a lysosome, which places the channel inside macroautophagy. Fission is the other half of the same cycle, since a lysosome consumed by fusion has to be regenerated by tubulation and scission from the hybrid organelle, and a calcium source in that membrane is what times it, so the channel contributes to lysosome organization. When the acceptor membrane is the plasma membrane instead of another organelle, the same burst under a docked lysosome drives it to fuse outward, giving calcium ion regulated lysosome exocytosis and with it a route for repairing the cell surface and discharging luminal contents. The iron and zinc the pore also passes leave by the same opening, so the channel is the exit that keeps them from accumulating, which is intracellular iron ion homeostasis and intracellular zinc ion homeostasis.

A gating lipid has to be made and removed, so upstream I place the PIKFYVE kinase with its scaffold VAC14 and the phosphatase FIG4 that turns the product over, which makes the channel's output a readout of lipid-kinase signalling, never of a receptor. A calcium shell that thin disperses before it travels, so whatever reads it has to sit at the mouth of the pore: calmodulin, and the penta-EF-hand sensor ALG-2. ALG-2 is the one that converts the transient into movement, recruiting dynein and with it the tubulation and repositioning of the organelle. For fusion outward the sensors change, synaptotagmin-family proteins taking over and acting with SNAREs and Rab7-dependent tethering. That same transient lets the compartment report on itself through calcineurin, whose calcium-dependent dephosphorylation of the TFEB-family transcription factors turns it into organelle biogenesis, opposed by mTORC1 on the same membrane surface. The ring implies partners of another kind, since the paralogous mucolipins TRPML2 and TRPML3 fold the same module and should substitute into it, so the channel need not be homomeric and which mixture prevails the chain cannot settle. The cysteine cluster beside the carboxyl sorting signal needs an enzyme too, a ZDHHC-family acyltransferase to install the acyl chains that hold that tail against the bilayer. The proline-rich, acid-flanked segment at the insert's midpoint will be extended and non-globular, tethering the two halves of the cap, and an exposed linker in a lumen full of cathepsins is a scissile site, so proteolytic clipping bounds its working lifetime. Only the tetramer is obligate here; everything else meets the channel and leaves, so I count it a hub that a compartment's machinery visits, not a subunit of any one complex. Losing the conductance should present not as a transport defect but as a storage defect, material entering the lysosome and never leaving it.

- Summary: A six-transmembrane cation channel of the mucolipin family, built as a tetramer in the limiting membrane of late endosomes and lysosomes. Four subunits contribute a re-entrant pore loop whose paired aspartates form an acidic constriction, giving a cation-selective conduit biased toward divalents but not strictly calcium-selective, so it releases luminal calcium down a steep gradient into the cytosol and passes ferrous iron and zinc by the same route. It is gated from the cytosolic face by phosphatidylinositol 3,5-bisphosphate, bound at a basic patch preceding the first membrane helix and coupled allosterically to a gate on the last helix, and modulated from the lumen by a tetrameric extracytosolic cap whose acidic and histidine-rich surface titrates over the pH range that separates an acidifying organelle from the cytoplasm. The calcium it releases acts locally on fusion and fission sensors, driving endolysosomal fusion, autophagosome-lysosome fusion, lysosome reformation and calcium-triggered lysosomal exocytosis, and feeding a calcium-dependent transcriptional arm that regulates organelle biogenesis. Acidic dileucine sorting signals at the extreme ends of the chain, plus an adjacent palmitoylated cysteine cluster, hold the protein in the endolysosomal system and retrieve it from the plasma membrane, and an exposed proline-rich linker in the luminal cap makes it a substrate for luminal proteases.
